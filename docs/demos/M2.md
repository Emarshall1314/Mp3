# M2 demo: Hi-res engine

Run this on a Linux desktop with PipeWire (or PulseAudio) and, ideally, a USB DAC. Build and start
the app as in M1 (`pnpm dev`, or a built package). Use the fixture library (`cargo xtask fixtures`,
then add `fixtures-out/formats` as a music folder) or your own files.

## 1. Signal path, shared output
1. Play a 24-bit/96 kHz FLAC (`flac_s24_96000` in the fixtures).
2. Click the quality badge in the player bar. Expected:
   - Source: `FLAC · 24-bit/96 kHz · 2 ch`.
   - Output: `Shared · 32-bit float · <server rate>`. A Resampled step appears if the sound server
     runs at another rate.
   - Not bit-perfect: "Shared output: the system mixer can change the samples."

## 2. Exclusive, bit-perfect
1. Open the output menu (the speaker button in the player bar). Your DAC is listed as
   `Exclusive · bit-perfect capable · hw:CARD=…`. Choose it.
2. Playback continues on the DAC from the same point.
   - PipeWire/PulseAudio give the card up through the D-Bus device reservation.
   - Other apps can't use the DAC until you switch back.
3. The player bar shows **Bit-perfect**. The signal path shows `Exclusive · 24-bit integer · 96 kHz`.
4. Play a 44.1 kHz track next. The device switches rate between the tracks; that is the only gap.
5. Lower the volume below 100%. Bit-perfect goes away, and the panel says why. Return it to 100%.
6. If the DAC is in use by another program that won't release it, playing shows an error naming the
   reason. Cadenza does not fall back silently.

## 3. DSD
1. Play `dsf_dsd64`:
   - If the DAC driver offers native DSD, the signal path shows `DSD64 · native` and Bit-perfect.
   - Otherwise it shows `DSD64 · converted to PCM`, at 176.4 kHz.
2. For a DoP-capable DAC: in the output menu, tick **Send DSD as DoP**, then replay. Expected:
   `DSD64 · as DoP`, Bit-perfect, and the DAC's DSD indicator lights.
   - Do not enable DoP for a DAC that doesn't support it: it plays loud noise.
3. Play two DSD tracks on the System Default output. They convert to PCM and play gaplessly.

## 4. ReplayGain
1. Go to Sound (sidebar), ReplayGain, and choose **Auto**.
2. Tracks without ReplayGain tags are measured in the background ("Measuring loudness: n of N").
3. Play an album, then a single track from another album. The signal path shows the gain: album gain
   for the album, track gain for the single.
4. Raise the Preamp to +12 dB with Prevent clipping on. The gain stops where the track's peak would
   clip.

## 5. EQ, crossfeed, convolution, limiter
1. On Sound, tick **Equalizer** and drag a graphic band. The curve follows, and you hear the change
   within a moment.
2. Choose **Parametric**, then **Import AutoEq…**: pick a `ParametricEQ.txt` from the AutoEq project
   for your headphones. The bands and preamp load.
3. Tick **Crossfeed** on headphones. A hard-panned recording sounds less separated.
4. Under **Convolution**, choose an impulse-response WAV. A missing or unreadable file shows an error
   and the rest of the chain keeps running.
5. Tick **Limiter**, then boost the EQ by +12 dB. Nothing clips.
6. Switch outputs. Each output has its own processing (headphones vs speakers).
7. The signal path lists every active stage, and Bit-perfect is off while any of them is on.

## 6. Crossfade
1. Set Crossfade to 6 s. Play a queue of singles from different albums: they overlap by 6 s.
2. Play an album: its tracks stay gapless (no fade).

## Automated checks
`cargo xtask ci` runs all of the above that needs no ears and no hardware:
- `core/engine/tests/engine.rs`: bit-perfect at every depth; DoP markers and payload; native DSD;
  gapless DSD on a non-DSD output; output switching; DSP; convolution with an IR file; ReplayGain
  modes; crossfade.
- `core/engine/tests/alsa.rs`: the real ALSA code path, writing through the `file` plugin and
  comparing the bytes with the decoded file (16/24/32-bit PCM and DSD).
- `core/api/tests/api.rs`: EBU R128 measurement of an untagged album, then playback at album gain.
